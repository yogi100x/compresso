# Compresso Plan Audit — Round 3 (2026-07-21, post-remediation verification)

Round 2 (five-pass audit, full text in git history at commit `7f089f3`) reported: 18/18 round-1 findings resolved, then raised 2 critical, 12 high, 12 medium, 9 low findings. A remediation pass (Codex session) followed: decisions D49–D64, new epics E15/E16, backlog grown to 204 tickets, file renames, ~620 changed lines. This round verifies that remediation finding-by-finding with three independent verification passes, evidence-checked against document text.

## Verdict

**Substantially improved; not yet closed.** Of round 2's 24 critical/high/medium findings: **10 fully resolved, 12 partially resolved, 0 unresolved** (2 covered without a recorded decision). Both criticals are fixed or nearly fixed. The dominant residual pattern: **fixes were applied as "amendment" bullets while the original ticket/spec bodies were left contradicting them** — an implementer reading a ticket body sees the old, wrong contract. 15 new defects were introduced by the remediation itself, one of them a circular dependency.

Slice status: E1–E6 implementable now. E7 implementable once fixture seed names are reconciled (P1 residue). The in-place-edit sweep below is roughly a day of editorial work, not redesign.

## Root cause and required practice

Nearly every PARTIAL above shares one cause: **remediation was written as an "Audit amendments" overlay instead of editing the authoritative ticket/spec bodies.** Amendments that contradict un-edited bodies are worse than either alone — two sources of truth, opposite contracts (E5-06 eager-vs-lazy is the sharpest case: the body's test asserts the amendment's behavior FAILS). Required practice going forward: amendments may announce a change, but the body text must be edited in the same pass; grep for the superseded wording before closing.
